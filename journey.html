My journey

From an early age, I was deeply sensitive to the inner realms of feeling, energy, and human connection.

When I was 16, my mother became ill with cancer, and I found myself naturally drawn toward hands-on healing. Although I didn’t fully understand what I was doing at the time, I felt an instinctive connection to channel divine universal energy for her healing.

A few years later, I became interested in Buddhist teachings, contemplative practice, and I began exploring how suffering can be transformed through awareness, compassion, and embodiment. Over the years, this path has led me to many silent meditation retreats and deepened my relationship to mindfulness, Buddhist psychology, and the journey of awakening.

I’ve also been deeply influenced by the work of teacher and mentor Sarah Powers, whose integration of Buddhism, Taoism, yoga, and transpersonal psychology opened a doorway into a more embodied and holistic understanding of healing and human experience.

In my early twenties, I met a Mayan healer, which sparked a deep curiosity in working with the elements and the body’s innate capacity for healing. This eventually led me to Mexico, where I was first introduced to acupuncture and Chinese Medicine. Shortly after, I returned to England to complete a degree in Chinese Medicine and Acupuncture.

After graduating, I continued my studies in Classical Chinese Medicine, Tui Na (Chinese bodywork), Reiki, and somatic approaches to healing – allowing my work to evolve into an integrative practice that bridges body, mind, emotion, and spirit.

What I love most about Chinese Medicine is its understanding that we aren’t separate from nature, but deeply interconnected with the world around us. It sees each person as unique, whole, and constantly in relationship with the greater rhythms of life and the wider whole.

This understanding continues to shape how I meet people in my work – with care, curiosity, presence, and a deep respect for each person’s own unfolding path.

Over the last decade, my connection to Hinduism, Bhakti, and devotional practice has continued to deepen and profoundly shape my life path. Through kirtan, mantra, and prayer, I’ve found a growing devotion to the path of love, service, and remembrance of God.

This led me on a life-changing pilgrimage to India in 2024, where I immersed myself in the teachings of Bhakti and Kriya yoga, and connected more deeply with the roots of my teachers lineage.

What continues to unfold in my heart is a commitment to living with greater presence, love, and reverence for life. More and more, I’m drawn to the understanding that we are souls on an evolutionary journey having a human experience; remembering that we are both human and divine.

To me, the essence of this path is a shared journey of awakening, remembrance and becoming – learning how to walk in love, as love.

Over the years, my work has naturally expanded to include somatic therapy, and have recently completed a two year somatic psychotherapy training with the Hakomi Institute. I also offer mindfulness and meditation coaching, women’s work & circles, and group spaces rooted in embodiment and contemplative practices.

Alongside Hakomi and somatic work, I also draw from approaches such as IFS (Internal Family Systems), compassionate inquiry, and mindfulness-based practices to support healing, self-understanding, and a deeper connection to our true nature.

At the heart of my work is a deep curiosity in how we can meet suffering with awareness, compassion, and presence – which is the doorway to our deepest freedom. Again and again, I’ve found that healing begins when we slow down enough to feel what’s here, and to gently meet ourselves as we are.

Through this process, many people begin to experience greater clarity, freedom, connection, and a deeper sense of belonging within themselves and their lives.
